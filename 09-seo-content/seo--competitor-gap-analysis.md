# Competitor Keyword Gap Analysis

A gap analysis finds keywords your competitors rank for that you don't. It's the fastest way to identify high-value content opportunities because the demand is already proven — someone is already ranking for the keyword — and the work is finding why you're missing from that conversation. This is prioritization research, not keyword discovery from scratch.

## Ahrefs Content Gap Tool

Ahrefs Content Gap (under Site Explorer → Competitors) is the standard tool for this. Input:

- Your domain in the "This target" field
- 2–4 competitors in the "But these competitors don't rank" fields

The output is a list of keywords where at least one competitor ranks in the top 10 and you do not. Sort by traffic potential descending, then filter by your difficulty threshold.

Semrush's Keyword Gap tool works the same way. Both are useful; pick whichever you have access to. The logic is identical: set difference between their ranking keywords and yours.

## Manual SERP Comparison

Without a paid tool, do this manually for your most important topics:

1. Identify 3–5 competitors via Google (search your core service keywords, note who ranks consistently)
2. Search each core keyword and list pages that rank in the top 5 that aren't yours
3. Use each competitor's URL in Ahrefs Free (limited queries), Ubersuggest, or Google's `site:` operator to estimate their topic coverage
4. For each competitor page you find, check: do you have a page targeting the same or equivalent keyword?

This is slower but surfaces the most important gaps — the ones on head terms — without needing a subscription.

## Prioritizing Gaps by Volume × Difficulty

Not all gaps are worth closing. A keyword gap where the competitor ranks for 10,000 monthly searches at KD 85 (very competitive) isn't actionable unless you're already a high-authority domain. Prioritize by:

**Score = Monthly Search Volume × (1 / Keyword Difficulty)**

This simple formula surfaces keywords where demand is real but competition hasn't locked it down. Normalize both variables (0–1 scale) if you want a single comparable score across your list.

Also consider: keyword gaps in your existing topic clusters are more actionable than gaps in new topics. Closing a gap in a cluster where you already have topical authority requires less new content than starting from scratch in an unfamiliar topic area.

## The Strike Zone: Medium Volume + Medium Difficulty

The most productive keywords for most sites live in what practitioners call the "strike zone": roughly 500–5,000 monthly searches and KD 20–50. These keywords are:

- Achievable without months of link building
- Specific enough that ranking earns qualified traffic
- Competitive enough that there are existing ranking pages to study and improve upon

Keywords below 200 searches/month have diminishing returns unless you're building programmatic pages at scale. Keywords with KD > 70 require domain authority you may not have. Above 10,000 searches, competition is usually entrenched — these are goals, not near-term opportunities.

The strike zone shifts by site authority. A new site's strike zone is KD 10–30. An established site's is KD 40–65. Calibrate to your actual DR/DA.

## Turning Gaps into Content

For each gap keyword:

1. Check if you have an existing page that should rank but doesn't (ranking problem — fix the page)
2. Check if the keyword fits naturally into an existing cluster (expansion — add a section or spin up a supporting page)
3. If neither, assess whether the topic is relevant to your site's authority — if it's off-topic, deprioritize regardless of volume

Never create content solely to fill a gap. The gap analysis identifies opportunity; content strategy determines whether to pursue it. A gap in a topic you can't write with genuine expertise is not a gap worth closing.

## Key Rules

- Use Ahrefs Content Gap or equivalent; run against 3–5 real SERP competitors, not aspirational brands
- Prioritize gaps using a volume × inverse-difficulty score
- Target the strike zone: ~500–5,000 monthly searches, KD 20–50 (adjust by site authority)
- Close gaps within existing clusters first — topical authority compounds
- Check whether a gap is a missing page or a ranking failure before creating new content
- Never create a page solely to check a gap box — content must have genuine depth and relevance
