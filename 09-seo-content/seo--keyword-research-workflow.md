# SEO: Keyword Research Workflow

## Overview
Keyword research answers: what do people actually search for, what do they want when they search it, and which opportunities are winnable given your site's current authority? Most keyword research mistakes come from targeting the wrong intent (creating a blog post for a query that wants a product page), pursuing impossible competition (going after head terms with zero authority), or creating one page per keyword instead of clustering related queries on a single strong page.

## Step 1 — Seed Keywords

Start with business-level terms: what does the site do, sell, or help with? Don't filter yet — generate a raw list.
- Product/service names
- Problems the business solves
- Industry terminology + customer vocabulary (customers say "cheap flights", not "low-cost air travel")
- Competitor brand names (comparative intent)

## Step 2 — Expansion

Feed seeds into keyword tools and collect volume + difficulty data:
- **Ahrefs / SEMrush**: most comprehensive keyword databases, best for competitive analysis
- **Google Keyword Planner**: accurate volume buckets, useful for PPC but can be used for SEO
- **GSC Performance report**: shows what queries already drive impressions — highest-intent seeds
- **"People Also Ask" boxes + related searches**: SERP-native intent signals

Collect 500–2000 raw keywords. Don't filter yet.

## Step 3 — Intent Classification

For each keyword cluster, determine search intent:
- **Informational**: user wants to learn ("how to change oil", "what is LCP")
- **Commercial investigation**: user is comparing options ("best accounting software", "X vs Y")
- **Transactional**: user wants to buy/act now ("buy running shoes", "schedule oil change")
- **Navigational**: user wants a specific site ("Stripe login", "Ahrefs dashboard")

Intent determines page type:
- Informational → blog post, guide, FAQ
- Commercial investigation → comparison page, review, roundup
- Transactional → product/service landing page with clear CTA

**Do not mismatch intent**: a landing page for "how to" query will not rank. A blog post for "buy [product]" query will not convert.

## Step 4 — Keyword Clustering

Do not create one page per keyword. Group keywords that share the same intent and SERP results:
- Search both keywords → if the top 5 results are the same pages, same cluster
- 3 variations targeting the same page = one page with all 3 variations covered naturally

## Step 5 — Prioritization

Score each cluster on three axes:
1. **Volume**: monthly search volume (use realistic estimates — Ahrefs volume tends to be lower than actual)
2. **Difficulty (KD)**: keyword difficulty score + manual SERP check (are top results DA 80+ sites?)
3. **Business value**: transactional keywords > commercial > informational

Prioritize: high volume + low KD + high business value. Be realistic about domain authority — targeting KD 60+ with a new site results in no traffic.

## Step 6 — Map to Content Plan

Assign each keyword cluster to a page type and URL. Existing pages first (add keywords to existing content before creating new pages). New pages ranked by ROI.

## Key Rules

- One page per intent cluster — not one page per keyword
- Always check SERP before committing to a keyword — the page types ranking reveal what Google thinks the intent is
- Low volume ≠ low value: "hire software developer San Francisco" gets 50 searches/month but each is worth thousands
- KD scores are proxies — manual SERP analysis is the final word on difficulty
- GSC Performance report is the highest-signal starting point for existing sites — these are already-proven queries
- Refresh keyword research every 6–12 months; demand patterns shift
