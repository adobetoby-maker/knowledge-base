# SEO: Link Building Strategy

## Overview
Backlinks remain one of the strongest ranking signals because they represent editorial votes of confidence from other publishers. The challenge is that links are hard to earn at scale — low-effort tactics (link farms, PBNs, paid links) carry algorithmic and manual penalty risk. Sustainable link building means creating content worth linking to, then making sure the right people know it exists.

## Link Quality Signals

A link's value is determined by:
- **Domain Authority**: links from established, trusted domains carry more signal than new/low-traffic sites
- **Topical Relevance**: a link from a site in the same industry is weighted more than one from an unrelated site
- **Page-level authority**: the specific page linking to you matters — a link from a site's homepage or high-traffic resource page outperforms a link from a buried archive page
- **Link placement**: editorial links in body copy > sidebar/footer links (often devalued by Google)
- **Anchor text**: descriptive and varied — excessive exact-match anchor text is a spam signal

## Earning Editorial Links

**Linkable asset creation**: build resources that solve a problem other publishers want to reference
- Original research / survey data (journalists and bloggers cite statistics)
- Free tools (calculators, generators, checklists)
- Comprehensive guides that replace scattered sources
- Unique datasets or visualizations

The asset must exist before outreach. "We have a guide" → no links. "We have the definitive guide that answers [specific question]" → some links.

## Competitor Backlink Replication

1. Pull competitor's backlink profile (Ahrefs, Majestic, Moz)
2. Filter for editorial links (exclude directories, forums, blogrolls)
3. Identify patterns: which types of content earn links in this niche?
4. Build similar or better content
5. Outreach to the same sites: "You linked to [competitor's resource] — we built an updated version with [specific improvement]"

This is the most efficient link building tactic because demand is already validated.

## Broken Link Building

1. Find broken outbound links on high-authority pages in your niche
2. Check if you have (or can create) a replacement resource
3. Notify the webmaster: "Found a broken link on [page] — we have a resource that covers the same topic"
4. Conversion rate: 5–10% — low but entirely passive after initial build

## Digital PR

- Publish studies, surveys, or trend reports with a newsworthy angle
- Pitch to journalists via HARO/Connectively, Qwoted, or direct outreach
- Newsworthiness: surprising finding, contrast with common belief, data with local angle
- Best outcome: editorial mention in major publications with high DA links and brand exposure

## Guest Posts

- Acceptable on genuinely relevant sites with real readership
- Link in the bio or editorially within content — both pass value
- Avoid: low-quality "write for us" directories, sites that accept any submission, sites with no editorial standards
- Google's stance: guest posts for links violate guidelines; for traffic/audience, they're fine. In practice, quality guest posts on relevant sites continue to pass value.

## What to Avoid

- **Link farms**: networks of low-quality sites created to sell links — algorithmic penalty risk
- **Private Blog Networks (PBNs)**: expired domain networks used to build links — high penalty risk if detected
- **Paid links without nofollow/sponsored tag**: violates Google guidelines, risks manual action
- **Link exchanges ("I'll link to you if you link to me")**: reciprocal links are devalued
- **Irrelevant directory submissions**: waste of time, no real signal value

## Key Rules

- 10 links from relevant, mid-authority sites > 100 links from irrelevant low-quality sites
- Anchor text diversity matters: brand name, URL, partial match, generic ("here", "source") — all-exact-match triggers spam filters
- Disavow only as a last resort for demonstrably harmful links (manual action received)
- Track link acquisition in a spreadsheet: target URL, your page, date, DR/DA, anchor text
- Velocity matters: a sudden spike of 500 links looks manipulated — natural growth is gradual
